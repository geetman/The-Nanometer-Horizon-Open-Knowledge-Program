# Preface
Hey everyone! This is Krishaang Kaushik, aka NMCKK or Geetman, CEO of nanometer Horizon<sup>TM</sup>. This repository is the central hub for all public repositories created under the Open Knowledge Program. It does not contain a single project or codebase. Instead, it exists as a navigation index, linking to independently maintained repositories that share a common goal.
```
All knowledge must be free, accessible, and approachable.
```
The Open Knowledge Program "aka: NMOKP" is an effort to collect, organize, and distribute educational resources without paywalls, artificial barriers, or unnecessary complexity. Every repository linked here is designed to be understandable, reusable, and open to everyone, regardless of background or prior experience.

## Purpose of This Repository
- Acts as a single entry point to all Open Knowledge Program repositories
- Organizes projects by topic, language, and purpose
- Links to repositories with their own focused documentation
- Keeps the ecosystem discoverable and navigable
- This repository is intentionally lightweight. The depth lives in the linked projects.

## What You’ll Find Here
- Repositories connected to this hub may include:
- Language learning resources and reference collections
- Educational tools and learning aids
- Structured guides, notes, and explanations
- Open datasets and reference material
- Each repository stands alone, but together they form a growing, interconnected knowledge network.

## Open Contribution Philosophy
- The Open Knowledge Program values clarity over complexity.
- Knowledge should be easy to follow
- Explanations should assume curiosity, not expertise
- Improvements that increase accessibility are always welcome
- Contributions are encouraged where they make learning clearer, more accurate, or more inclusive.

# University Browsing
## What is this?
Many universities host course materials — lecture notes, slides, problem sets, and readings — on publicly accessible web pages, with no login or paywall required. Unlike formal open courseware programs (MIT OCW, NPTEL, etc.), these are often just professor personal pages or department directories that happen to be publicly indexed. The content is the same material given to enrolled students, just sitting openly on a university server.

## How to do it?
The core trick is **reverse-engineering URLs**. When you find a useful PDF from a university, strip back the URL path to find the course directory.

**Example:**
```
https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~mjb/cs557/Handouts/glman.pdf
↓ remove the filename
https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~mjb/cs557/Handouts/
↓ remove the subfolder
https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~mjb/cs557/
```
You've now landed on the full course page with all materials.

**Finding PDFs to start from:**
- Google: `site:*.edu filetype:pdf "your subject"`
- Google: `site:university.edu filetype:pdf "lecture notes"`
- Google: `site:university.edu/~professorname` to find all their courses

**Common URL patterns to look for:**
- `web.university.edu/~username/coursecode/` — professor personal pages
- `cs.university.edu/courses/cs101/` — department course directories  
- `people.university.edu/~username/` — faculty pages often have multiple courses

**Note:** This works best with older professor personal pages. Many modern university systems lock directories or require login, so results will vary.

**Using a university's course catalog to browse systematically:**

Some universities publish their full course catalog publicly. You can use this to identify courses by subject, find the course code, then search for open materials using that code.

[Oregon State Course Catalog](https://classes.oregonstate.edu) is a good example of this workflow:
1. Browse the catalog by subject to find a course that interests you
2. Note the course code (e.g. `CS 557`)
3. Search Google: `site:oregonstate.edu "CS 557" lecture notes` or browse `web.engr.oregonstate.edu` for that code
4. Reverse-engineer the URL from any PDF you find

Many universities publish similar public catalogs — look for a `classes.` or `catalog.` subdomain on the university's main domain.

## University List
Universities with strong openly accessible course materials but **without** a dedicated OCW program:

[Oregon State University](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu) - Engineering, Computer Graphics, and CS faculty pages that are very openly accessible with full course directories.
 
[University of Sydney](https://www.sydney.edu.au) - Engineering, Science, and Medicine faculty pages often publicly accessible, especially useful once enrolled.

[Stanford](https://cs.stanford.edu) - Individual CS, AI, and Math course pages outside their official paid platform, many with slides and assignments.
 
[UC Berkeley](https://www.eecs.berkeley.edu) - EECS, Math, and Physics department pages with open materials beyond the official recordings.
 
[University of Toronto](https://www.cs.toronto.edu) - CS, AI, and Robotics faculty pages that are frequently open and well-maintained.
 
[ETH Zurich](https://ethz.ch/en) - Engineering, Physics, and Math lecture notes frequently publicly hosted by faculty.
 
[TU Delft](https://www.tudelft.nl/en) - Engineering and Aerospace department pages with strong open materials alongside their formal OCW program.
 
[University of Cambridge](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk) - Mathematics and Physics department famous for openly hosted lecture notes, particularly in maths.
 
[Imperial College London](https://www.imperial.ac.uk) - Engineering, Physics, and Medicine department pages that are often open and high quality.

> **Tip:** When browsing a university, start with the department page rather than the main site. Engineering, CS, and Mathematics departments are the most likely to have openly accessible course directories.
